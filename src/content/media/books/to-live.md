Through the vicissitudes of Fugui's life, Yu Hua offers a quiet gaze at human resilience; the novel was first published in 1993. The cover used here is from the Writers Publishing House edition.

## To Live Is Not a Conclusion

This is not a novel that piles suffering on the reader and then demands tears. Yu Hua lets Fugui lose everything while keeping the narrative voice level: things happen, and the person keeps living. It is precisely this restraint — refusing to inflate emotion on the reader's behalf — that makes every farewell harder to escape.

Fugui and the old ox sitting together in the dusk feels like the breath the novel leaves behind. It offers no answers, nor does it dress up living as a victory; living simply means still being able to eat, talk, keep company, and see tomorrow after loss.
