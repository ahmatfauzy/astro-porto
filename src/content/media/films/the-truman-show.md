Directed by Peter Weir and written by Andrew Niccol, Jim Carrey plays Truman Burbank, whose life is broadcast live around the clock without his knowledge. It places media watching, free will, and everyday life on the same set.

## Beyond the Door

The film's most moving quality is not that it predicted reality TV or social media, but that Truman finally treats the familiar as a question. The town's weather, the neighbors' small talk, the edges of fear — all were carefully arranged into a sense of safety.

When he sails toward the edge of the sea, the door does not guarantee freedom; it only gives the choice back to him, for the first time. That ending is both light and a little sharp: to leave a watched life, you first have to admit you were being watched.
